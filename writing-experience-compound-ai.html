How Do We Make Experience Compound with AI?

This is a relatively short article because I'm still consolidating my thoughts here.

Modern AI consumes extraordinary amounts of data. People often claim that they need or want "more data" to make models better as if it will solve all of their problems. Yet, it amazes me how humans can learn so much with so little and can often ace a test with a healthy snack (i.e., an orange, hummus and carrots, idk cheese) that doesn't consume as much energy as an H100.

Humans don't memorize examples. We turn them into reusable ideas. A child learns that objects fall, then applies those principles to stacking books, catching balls, and eventually understanding what gravity is (maybe not in terms of -9.81 m/s^2, but observationally). One lesson modifies those future subsequent behaviors.

I feel like AI systems don't do this that well currently. A model can be corrected in a conversation and then repeat that same mistake in the next. When I have several Claude Code sessions open, I feel like I'm managing several different kids: feeding one, putting the other to sleep, changing one's diapers (of course, metaphorically). I.e., I'm steering each one to minimize the gap between what I clearly want the agent to do and what the agent is explicitly accomplishing.

A company can spend weeks teaching an AI its workflow, yet the knowledge often remains trapped inside prompts, temporary context, or a specialized fine-tune. A truly adaptive system would identify useful skills, connect it to existing knowledge, test whether it transfers, and preserve it without removing other abilities. These models should be easy to teach and smart to pick up on and transfer skills, often in a much more sample efficient manner.

Human intelligence is powerful not just because we learn from less data, but because we actually accumulate and transfer skills across multiple domains. I think the future of AI will depend on building systems that can learn in a more human way: i.e., learning to extract more signal from less, pick up useful skills, and let experience actually compound.
