how do we make experience compound with ai?

This is a relatively short article because I'm still consolidating my thoughts here.

Modern AI consumes extraordinary amounts of data. People often claim that they need or want "more data" to make models better as if it will solve all of their problems. Yet, that doesn't make sense to me. When learning how to drive a car, we don't ingest a massive dataset that says, Teslas are trained on. We learn rules and we actively deploy those rules pretty quickly.

Furthermore, it amazes me how humans can learn so much with so little and can often ace a test with a healthy snack (i.e., an orange, hummus and carrots, idk cheese) that doesn't consume as much energy as an H100.

Humans don't memorize examples. We turn them into reusable ideas. A child learns that objects fall, then applies those principles to stacking books, catching balls, and eventually understanding what gravity is (maybe not in terms of -9.81 m/s^2, but observationally). Whenever I'm at the grocery store or at a park in public, I marvel at kids: they're constantly trying, observing "rewards," and learning. They learn by doing and that experience sticks with them. One lesson modifies those future subsequent behaviors. They "continually" learn.

I feel like AI systems don't do this that well currently, but I am optimistic that they will. For example, a model can be corrected in a conversation and then repeat that same mistake in the next. When I have several Claude Code sessions open, I feel like I'm managing several different kids: feeding one, putting the other to sleep, changing one's diapers (of course, metaphorically). I.e., I'm steering each one to minimize the gap between what I clearly want the agent to do and what the agent is explicitly accomplishing. Broadly, I think that these models can get really good at generalist tasks, but may struggle to truly hold a memorable and continual experience that shapes their future rationale. We're not far from achieving this philosophy of enabling models to actually learn like humans and I am confident that we will reach there someday.

To the point of AI systems learning, a company can spend weeks teaching an AI its workflow, yet the knowledge often remains trapped inside prompts, temporary context, or a specialized fine-tuning approach. A truly adaptive system would identify useful skills, connect it to existing knowledge and skills, test whether it transfers to other domains, and preserve it without removing other abilities (i.e., learn when to use which lessons and which lessons are the most useful). These models should be easy to teach and smart to pick up on and transfer skills, often in a much more sample efficient manner. There's also the factor of achieving greater intelligence throughput from these models by optimizing calculations, etc.

Human intelligence is powerful not just because we learn from less data, but because we actually accumulate and transfer skills across multiple domains. We learn how to write and we apply that in our daily lives in several different contexts. We learn how to speak and malleably shape this accordingly depending on who we're talking to and what we're talking about. But, when I'm speaking to the cashier at a grocery store, I don't manually pull a Claude skill or pause and run inference on the entirety of Whole Foods' history — I just know (maybe an arbitrary example). I think the future of AI will depend on building systems that can learn in a more human way: i.e., learning to extract more signals from less, pick up useful skills, and let experience actually compound.
